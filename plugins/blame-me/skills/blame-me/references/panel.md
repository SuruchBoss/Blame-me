# Choosing the panel

The panel is the people who will really judge the work. A good panel has the person who decides, the person who pays or has to live with the result, and one seat the user is likely to forget.

Propose seats, then let the user change them. Users often know their real reader better than any default. A small business owner's real "finance" reader may be the accountant, not a finance director. A student's real reader may be one teacher, not a panel of investors.

Match the seats to the user. A student entering a school contest does not need a venture investor; a founder raising money does not need a teacher.

For each seat write three things:

- **Who:** the role.
- **Looks at first:** the two or three things they check before anything else.
- **Deciding question:** the one question that becomes their Pass or Fail.

## Student work

**School or university project, report, or presentation**
- Teacher or advisor. Looks at: does it answer the assignment, is the reasoning sound, are sources real. Deciding question: "Does this meet what I asked for?"
- A classmate seeing it for the first time. Deciding question: "Do I understand what this is about from the first page?"
- Someone who would use the result (a shop owner, a club member, a family member). Deciding question: "Would this actually help me?"

**Contest entry or idea pitch (business plan contest, innovation contest, hackathon idea)**
- Contest judge. Looks at: the judging criteria, whether it can really be done, what is new about it. Deciding question: "Would I score this in the top group?"
- A classmate or friend who would be the customer. Deciding question: "Would I pay for this, or use it, next week?"
- Someone who already runs something similar (a stall owner, a small shop). Deciding question: "Does this match how this really works?"

**Small business idea (selling at school, online, at a market)**
- A friend who would be the customer. Deciding question: "Would I buy this at this price?"
- The person who would lend or give the money (a parent, a relative, a teacher running a fund). Deciding question: "Will I get my money back, and when?"
- Someone who sells something similar. Deciding question: "What goes wrong in the first week?"

**Scholarship or university application, personal statement**
- Selection committee member. Deciding question: "Out of a pile of these, would this one stay on the table?"
- Teacher who knows what the committee wants. Deciding question: "Does it answer the question they asked?"
- A reader who knows nothing about the applicant. Deciding question: "Can I see what this person has actually done?"

## Professional work

**Pitch deck asking for money**
- Early-stage investor. Deciding question: "Would I put money into this at this stage?"
- Target customer (the one who pays). Deciding question: "Would I pay for this next month?"
- Someone who has run a similar business. Deciding question: "Does this match how this business really works?"

**Business plan or idea for your own business**
- Target customer. Deciding question: "Would I pay, and what would I stop using?"
- Accountant. Looks at: profit on each sale, when money comes in and goes out, costs left out. Deciding question: "Does each sale make or lose money?"
- Someone who has done something similar. Deciding question: "What breaks first in month three?"

**Proposal to a client or to management**
- The person who signs. Deciding question: "Would I approve this as written?"
- Finance or purchasing. Deciding question: "Is the cost clear, fair, and complete?"
- The team that would do the work or use the result. Deciding question: "Can we really run this?"

**Case study or take-home assignment for a job**
- Hiring manager. Deciding question: "Would I bring this person to the next round?"
- Future teammate. Deciding question: "Would this be useful to me, or would I have to redo it?"
- Recruiter reading for one minute. Deciding question: "From the first page, do I understand what this is and why it is good?"

**Resume or cover letter**
- Recruiter scanning quickly. Deciding question: "In thirty seconds, does this match the job?"
- Hiring manager. Deciding question: "Are the claims backed by results I can believe?"
- Someone who does this job today. Deciding question: "Does this person sound like they have done the work?"

**Article, post, or announcement**
- Target reader. Deciding question: "Would I read to the end and do something about it?"
- A reader who knows nothing about the topic. Deciding question: "Do I understand the point from the first paragraph?"
- Editor. Deciding question: "Is every claim supported and every sentence needed?"

## Size

- 3 seats by default. Add one only when it brings a view the others do not.
- At most 5.
- Never two seats that would ask the same deciding question.
